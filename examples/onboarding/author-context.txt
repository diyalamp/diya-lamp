All information in this exercise is fictional.

The 38% figure is 76 of 200 new accounts in the last four weeks. We have no earlier baseline or experiment. Of those 200 accounts, 80 arrived through a new advertising campaign aimed at very small teams; 12 of those 80 activated. The other 120 accounts produced 64 activations. We have not established why either group did or did not activate.

The three requests for self-serve setup came from experienced administrators. In six recent onboarding calls, four teams needed help importing technician availability from spreadsheets. Two needed help understanding the scheduling workflow. These are small convenience samples, not a representative survey.

Bulk import is the feature intended to address spreadsheet setup. Five existing paying customers requested it; one said renewal next quarter depends on it. That is their stated concern, not a guaranteed cancellation. We have not estimated its revenue impact.

The three-week wizard estimate covers implementation only. Instrumentation, accessibility checks, and migration work could add one to two weeks. Our two engineers cannot build the wizard and bulk import simultaneously. Current onboarding work occupies roughly 12 specialist hours per week. We have no estimate of the wizard's ongoing support burden.

A small pilot is possible: keep assisted onboarding available, invite 30 suitable new accounts to an optional wizard, and compare their experience with a contemporaneous assisted group. Assignment, eligibility, and measurement need to be designed before launch. A small pilot can reveal usability problems; it cannot reliably establish every segment's long-term business effect.

My goal is faster successful setup without hurting customers who need import assistance. I am willing to change the recommendation. Sixty percent activation was an aspiration, not a forecast supported by data.
